"Riftbound's Radiance Streeted Nine Days Ago -- and the 'Format Staple' Theory We Floated on Vendetta Didn't Survive It"

⚔️ Riftbound TCG 2026-11-01

Radiance, Riftbound's fifth main set, streeted worldwide on October 23 -- nine days old as of this writing, and the first Riftbound release to launch in English, French, and Simplified Chinese simultaneously. The theme is the League of Legends universe's in-game music groups, K/DA and Heartsteel, and Riot built an entire parallel Showcase print run around it: alongside the normal alt-art Signature treatment, several Heartsteel members get their own "Heartsteel Signature" and "Heartsteel Overnumbered" versions, stacked on top of the standard rarity ladder. It's a new wrinkle for the game, and the early pricing shows collectors are treating the two Signature lines very differently.

Riven's base-to-Signature spread is the number that jumps out. The plain version of the exact same card is $0.14 -- basically bulk. The Overnumbered (playable, no-premium) copy is $215.31. The full Signature alt-art is $1,282.53. That's not a typo-sized gap: it's roughly a 9,000x jump from base print to Signature, and about 6x again from Overnumbered to Signature on top of that. For a set that's barely a week and a half old, that's an unusually steep early ladder even by Riftbound's own thin-market standards.

The Heartsteel Signature tier is pricing well below the "real" Signature tier for now, and that's worth watching rather than assuming. Aphelios at $993.25 and Ezreal at $692.50 are real money, but they're sitting under Riven's and Seraphine's $1,000-1,100+ non-Heartsteel Signatures. Early in a set's life, a secondary parallel usually prices below the mainline chase treatment until collectors decide whether it's a permanent sub-line or a one-off gimmick -- Heartsteel's gap could close, widen, or stay put once more sales print and the market has more than a handful of listings to average.

Now, the follow-up we owed from September. Our Vendetta piece a month ago flagged Akali, Rogue Assassin's Overnumbered print climbing 33.6% in six weeks and asked whether that meant she was a genuine format staple -- and said the real test was whether that number held once Radiance actually streeted and attention moved to the new set. It didn't hold. Akali's Overnumbered print is down to $446.44 from $592.63 on September 27, a 24.7% drop in five weeks. Vendetta's sealed product cooled even harder: the booster display fell from $160.53 to $115.21 (-28.2%) and single packs from $7.10 to $5.26 (-25.9%). Interestingly, the Signature chase prints barely moved -- Zed's Signature is down 5.6% and Jayce's Signature down 5.9%, while Akali's Signature is sitting at exactly $3,229.71, unchanged to the penny from September, which in a market this thin usually means no new sales have actually printed rather than genuine price stability.

The pattern that's emerging: Signature chase prints hold their ground through a new-set launch; playable and sealed tiers don't. That tracks with what you'd expect if Signature buyers are collectors holding long and Overnumbered/sealed buyers are players and shops restocking for what's actually being played right now -- and right now, that's Radiance, not Vendetta.

Riftbound's Signature and Heartsteel Signature tiers are trading in genuinely thin volume on a set barely over a week old -- treat every Radiance number above as an early snapshot, not a settled valuation, and check current listings before buying or selling into these prices.
