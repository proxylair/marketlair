"Magic's Star Trek Set Streeted Two Days Ago -- and Its Cheapest Rares Are Out-Multiplying the Mythics in Surge Foil"

🔮 Magic: The Gathering 2026-11-15

Star Trek, Magic's newest Universes Beyond crossover, streeted worldwide on November 13 -- two days old as of this writing, so this is as close to launch-week pricing as it gets. The set introduces Surge Foil as a new premium treatment riding on top of the existing Showcase and Borderless frames, the same stacking trick Magic has used on recent crossover sets to create a scarcer-than-scarce chase tier. Two days in, the early numbers show something that doesn't show up in every set: ordinary Rares are out-multiplying the Mythics.

H.M.S. Bounty is the number that actually breaks the "rarity equals value" assumption. The plain printing is $0.30 -- a build-around Rare nobody is jamming into a competitive deck, pricing like bulk. The same card in Showcase Surge Foil is $245.00. That's roughly an 816x jump, and nothing about the card's power level changed between those two printings. It's the identical Oracle text on a piece of cardboard that happens to sit on the thinnest pull-rate in the set.

Will Riker and Dathon back up that H.M.S. Bounty isn't a fluke. Both are plain Rares sitting at $0.41 and $0.28 respectively -- as close to bulk as this set gets -- and both jump into triple digits in Surge Foil: $123.31 for Riker (about 300x) and $103.98 for Dathon (about 371x). Three different Rares, three different card names, the same story: when a treatment is scarce enough, the rarity letter printed on the card stops mattering.

Now compare those multiples to the set's actual Mythics, and the gap runs the other way. Picard goes from $8.29 plain to $240.65 in Surge Foil, about 29x. Spock goes from $9.31 to $168.65 in Borderless Surge Foil, about 18x. Kirk goes from $13.63 to $133.66, under 10x. The Mythics land at higher or comparable dollar prices to the Rares, but far smaller multiples over their own plain printing -- because a Mythic already has a real baseline price from people who actually want to cast it, while a $0.30 Rare has nothing holding it up except bulk-bin economics until Surge Foil scarcity takes over.

Sealed math is already pricing in a chase this concentrated. A Collector Booster Display Case is sitting at $2,513.68 two days post release, well above where a crossover set's collector product usually opens if the hit rate on these bonus-sheet foils were evenly spread. That kind of case price only holds if enough buyers believe a Surge Foil pull -- on a Mythic or a $0.30 Rare alike -- is worth chasing specifically for the treatment, not the card.

All prices above are live TCGplayer market-price snapshots pulled via TCGCSV on November 15, two days after Star Trek's November 13 street date. Surge Foil Showcase and Borderless copies are extremely thin-market at this stage -- few confirmed sales, sparse listings, and prices that can swing hard in either direction once more boxes get opened and population counts stabilize. Treat every number above as an early snapshot, not a settled valuation.
