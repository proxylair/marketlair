but presale singles and sealed product are already trading. That's early
enough that this data should be read as a snapshot of hype and thin supply,
not settled value -- but it's already telling a clear story about which
cards this set was built around.

**Mew ex and Mewtwo ex are numbered above the set's own card count, and
that's exactly why they're pricing four figures apart from everything
else.** 30th Celebration's base set runs 128 cards, but Mew ex and Mewtwo
ex are printed at 157/128 and 158/128 -- Pokemon's usual placement for the
one or two cards a whole anniversary set is designed to sell around, one
tier above even the Double Rare ex cards. That's the actual signal behind
the gap between a $3,333 Mew ex and a $69.99 Espeon ex: it isn't random
hype, it's the set's own numbering telling you where the real chase is.

**The identical $49.99 tier across wildly different rarities is a presale
artifact, not a demand read.** Greninja ex (a Double Rare), a random
Pikachu variant (one of 30 in the set), and a plain Common Eevee are all
sitting at the exact same $49.99 market price right now. That's what
happens when only a handful of sellers have listed a card presale and
nobody's actually sold one yet -- the price is a guess, not a market. Once
real sales start clearing after the September 16 street date, expect this
tier to fan back out hard: the Common will fall toward bulk, and Greninja
ex should hold real, separate value.

**Mewtwo ex's own numbers don't agree with each other, which is the
clearest tell of how thin this is.** Its market price is showing $1,000
while its low listing is actually higher, at $1,800 -- a market price
sitting below the cheapest active listing only happens when there are so
few completed sales that the "market" figure is being computed off stale
or sparse data. Treat that $1,000 as a floor estimate at best, not
something to transact against.

**Sealed product is carrying a heavy presale premium across the board.**
A 30th Celebration Elite Trainer Box at $184.60 is roughly triple what
Elite Trainer Boxes normally run at retail (usually $49.99 MSRP), and
the Ultra-Premium Collections at $600+ are well above their typical
$130-150 MSRP tier too. That's normal for a marquee anniversary release
in the ten days before street date, when the only supply is a small
number of early copies -- expect these numbers to compress, likely hard,
once wider retail stock (Target, Walmart, local shops) starts hitting
shelves at MSRP.

Net read: this is a set where the printed card number is doing more work
than the rarity stamp. If you're buying into 30th Celebration before it
streets, the Futuristic Rare pair is the real chase and priced like it
already -- everything else on this list is presale noise that hasn't
found its real level yet.

*All prices above are presale/pre-release market snapshots taken well
ahead of 30th Celebration's September 16 street date. This is thin,
low-volume data by definition -- completed sales are scarce, listings are
sparse, and prices (especially Mew ex, Mewtwo ex, and the sealed
Ultra-Premium Collections) can and likely will move sharply once the set
is actually in players' hands. Do not treat any number here as a stable
valuation.*
